Communication Review & Approval Process

Overview

01

This process governs how a USIC Academy communication gets from a request to an inbox. It mirrors the approval gate already built into the Academy Alert Email Template SOP and extends it in two directions: an intake stage at the front, where the requester submits content in the correct template, and a send-window gate at the back, held by USIC Communications.

There are two parties. The requester supplies the content. The LAT team builds the review email from it and drives every step after that. LAT does not write the content; requesters do not send the email.

The split is clean. The VP for Learning & Development owns whether the message goes and what it says. USIC Communications owns when it goes — the send window, and how it sequences against everything else hitting the same inboxes that week. Those are two different decisions, so they are two different approvals.

Communications approves the timing, not the message. By the time a send reaches Communications, the question of whether it goes out has already been settled by the VP. Communications is not a second content review and cannot veto a send — their approval is of the date and time. If they raise a concern about the content itself, that is feedback to carry back to the VP, not a gate.

When It Applies

02

This process applies to any LAT/L&D communication sent to a distribution group — Academy Alerts, course and program announcements, ILT invitations, nav and platform change notices, and general L&D communications.

In scopeAny send addressed to a company distribution group — from the moment a requester submits content through to delivery.
Out of scopeReplies to individuals, routine one-to-one or single-team correspondence, and Docebo system notifications generated by the platform.
Send-from mailboxUSICAcademy@usicllc.com — all USIC Academy communications send from this mailbox, without exception. Addressed To Undisclosed Recipients, with the distribution group in BCC.

Roles & Intake

03

Communications originate with a requester — a program owner, L&D manager, or anyone with content that needs to reach an audience. They submit it to LAT in the correct template. LAT builds the review email and carries it through approval and send.

RequesterSelects the correct template, supplies the content and the audience, and names a target send date. Owns what the message says.
LAT teamGenerates the review email from the submission, tiers it, routes it for approval, books the window, logs it, and sends. Owns how the message is built and delivered.
VP for Learning & DevelopmentApproves the message — whether it goes and what it says.
USIC CommunicationsApproves the send window — when it goes. Tier 2 and Tier 3 only.

Choosing the template

Two template families: USIC Academy, and the branded programs. The choice follows the content — whether this message belongs to a named program or to the Academy generally. It is not about which company brand the audience works for, and it is not about who is asking. Every one of these sends from USICAcademy@usicllc.com regardless of template.

USIC AcademyThe default. Use for anything not tied to a named program — platform and nav notices, Academy Alerts, course announcements, general L&D communications. Navy #00305B / green #94C83D, USIC Academy mark.
Branded programsThree, currently: Amplify, ULE, and the Ground Up. Use the program's own template when the message is that program's — its cohorts, its milestones, its content. A participant should recognise it as coming from the program they're in, not as generic Academy mail.
If it touches a program but isn't from itUse USIC Academy. An LMS change that happens to affect Amplify learners is an Academy notice, not an Amplify one.
If it spans two programsUse USIC Academy. Don't send the same message twice in two liveries.
Not a template axisCompany brand. Blood Hound, OnTarget, RECONN, and USIC audiences all receive Academy or program templates as normal — the subsidiary a recipient works for does not change the template family. Follow the brand guidelines for logo and palette within the chosen template where the audience warrants it.
If you're unsureAsk LAT before drafting. Rebuilding a submission in the other template costs more than the question does.

What a submission must contain

LAT cannot generate a review email from content alone. A submission is complete when it has all five:

1 · ContentIn the correct template, with every placeholder filled. No [brackets] remaining.
2 · AudienceWho must receive this, stated as a role or population — not a list address. LAT selects the list.
3 · Required actionOne sentence: who has to do what, by when. If it can't be written, the request isn't ready.
4 · Target send dateThe date you want it to land, and any hard deadline behind it.
5 · RequesterWho to come back to with questions, and who signs off on edits to the content.
Incomplete submissions go back, they don't go forward. LAT returns anything missing one of the five rather than guessing at it. A guessed audience is how a message reaches the wrong people with the right words.
Submission intake address and lead time are not yet set — see Open Items. Until they are, a target date is a request, not a commitment.

Step-by-Step

05
Phase 1 · Intake — requester
1

Select the correct template

Requester. Choose USIC Academy or your program template — Amplify, ULE, or the Ground Up — per Roles & Intake. The content decides, not who is asking. Unsure — ask LAT before you draft.

2

Submit the content to LAT

Requester. Send the completed template to LAT with all five elements from Roles & Intake: content, audience, required action, target send date, and requester. Anything missing comes back to you.

Phase 2 · Build — LAT team
3

Generate the review email

LAT. Build the review email from the submission — Outlook-safe table layout, inline CSS, images hosted on GitHub Pages, VML buttons for any CTA. Fill every placeholder. If the submission raises a question about meaning or scope, resolve it with the requester now, not after the VP has seen it.

4

Select the distribution list

LAT. Translate the requester's audience into a list — the narrowest one that reaches everyone who must act. Record which list and why you didn't go one wider. If no list matches the audience cleanly, go back to the requester before improvising.

5

Determine the approval tier

LAT. Look the list up in Approval Tiers. This sets whether the send date is yours to pick or has to be booked, and it sets the lead time. If the tier puts the requester's target date out of reach, tell them now.

6

Self-QC before anyone else looks at it

LAT. Send the draft to yourself. Check in classic Outlook desktop and on mobile: images render, links resolve, legible in dark mode. Reviewers should spend their attention on the message, not on catching broken images.

Phase 3 · Approval — LAT team
7

Get VP for Learning & Development review and approval

The fully drafted email must be reviewed and approved by the VP for Learning & Development. This gate applies at every tier.

Who approvesVP for Learning & Development
What to send for reviewThe finished draft with all placeholders filled in and no [brackets] remaining, plus the distribution list you intend to use and its tier.
How to requestForward the Outlook draft with APPROVAL NEEDED: at the start of the subject line, and wait for a written approval reply.
Lead timeUnconfirmed — see Open Items.
8

Get a send window from USIC Communications

Tier 2 and Tier 3 only. Once the VP has approved the message, ask USIC Communications to confirm the send date and time. The message itself is settled at this point — what you need from Communications is the slot. Do not add recipients or send until they have confirmed the window in writing.

Who approvesUSIC Communications — named contact / mailbox unconfirmed. See Open Items.
What they approveThe send window — date and time. Not the content, the audience, or whether the message goes.
PrerequisiteVP for Learning & Development approval already received, in writing.
What to send for reviewThe VP-approved draft, the distribution list and tier, your intended send date and time, and the one-sentence statement of who must act.
How to requestForward the draft with SEND WINDOW NEEDED: at the start of the subject line. Include the VP's written approval in the thread so Communications can see the content question is already closed.
What they're checkingCollision against other sends already booked for that audience, and sequencing — whether yours should land before or after something else in flight.
Lead timeUnconfirmed — see Open Items.
If they comment on contentTake it as feedback, not an instruction. Content is the requester's and the VP's — if you act on the comment, clear it with the requester and re-run Step 7. If you don't, proceed once the window is confirmed.
Communications may move your send to a different day to keep it clear of another company-wide message. That is the gate working as designed, not a rejection — build the possibility into your timeline rather than treating it as an exception. What they cannot do is decline the send.
9

Log the approvals

LAT. Record the send before it goes: date, distribution list, tier, subject line, template used, requester, VP approver and date, Communications approver and date. This is what makes “did we already tell them, who asked for it, and who signed off?” answerable three months later.

10

Add recipients and send

Send from USICAcademy@usicllc.com, addressed To Undisclosed Recipients, with the distribution group in BCC. Never send an Academy communication from a personal account — the from-address is what makes the message recognisable as Academy, and replies need to land somewhere the team can see them. Read the full list address aloud before you hit send — several of the field lists differ by two letters and mean very different things.

Emergency Bypass

06

The bypass exists for genuine emergencies — an active platform outage where waiting for sign-off would delay notifying learners about something that is affecting them right now. It does not exist for missed deadlines.

Emergency bypass: For a time-critical alert you may send immediately without prior approval, then forward the sent message to the VP for Learning & Development for retroactive review within 1 business day. Where the send was Tier 2 or Tier 3, copy USIC Communications on the same timeline — not for review, but so the send is on their calendar and they can sequence around it. An emergency sets its own timing; that is precisely why the window gate doesn't apply.
The 1-business-day window is carried over from the Academy Alert SOP so the two documents stay consistent. It governs the VP's retroactive review; for Communications it is just a notification deadline. If either is wrong, change it in both documents rather than letting them drift apart.

Tips

07
  • Tier the send early — the tier sets the lead time, and discovering it after the content is built is how target dates get missed.
  • A submission is not a send date. Tell requesters what the tier costs them in lead time when you receive the request, not when you miss their date.
  • Don't rewrite a requester's content. Build it, question it, send it back — but the words are theirs and the VP's, not LAT's.
  • Approvals are sequential, not parallel. Don't go to the VP and Communications at once to save a day — Communications shouldn't be booking a window for a message that may still change.
  • Put the tier and your target date in every request. Communications can't book a window you haven't named.
  • Written approval means written. A hallway “looks good” is not an approval you can log.
  • Narrow the list rather than soften the message. If a message feels too pointed for a wide audience, the audience is probably wrong.
  • A moved send is not a rejected send. Communications shifting your date to avoid a collision is the system working. They approve the window; the VP already approved the message.

Open Items

Intake addressWhere requesters submit content. USICAcademy@usicllc.com is the outbound mailbox — whether it doubles as intake, or whether submissions go to a form, a Jira queue, or a separate mailbox, is undecided. Owner: LAT.
Intake lead timeHow far ahead of a target date a submission must arrive, by tier. Without this, LAT absorbs every late request as its own problem. Owner: LAT / L&D leadership.
Template libraryWhere requesters get the Academy and program templates, and who maintains them. Owner: LAT.
Program template coverageWhether Amplify, ULE, and the Ground Up each have a built, current template. An Amplify template exists; ULE and the Ground Up are unconfirmed. Step 1 can't be followed for a program that has no template. Owner: LAT.

Requester sign-offWhether the requester sees and approves the built review email before it goes to the VP. Recommended — it is their content — but not yet decided. Owner: LAT.
Comms contactNamed approver or shared mailbox for USIC Communications — unknown. Owner: L&D leadership / USIC Communications.
Tier thresholdsThe Tier 1/2/3 cut lines are a proposal by LAT, not agreed policy. Communications may want a different boundary — e.g. Tier 3 only, or all field-technician sends regardless of size. Owner: USIC Communications.
